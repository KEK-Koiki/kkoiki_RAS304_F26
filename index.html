    Giving ordinary motors a sense of position.¶

    Elijah Koiki's motor encoder subsystem for the 2 DOF Smart Robot Arm Joint Showcase. Four optical channels turn the motion of two DC motor shafts into position and direction feedback for a distributed embedded control system.

    Explore the schematic → Follow the signal path

    Elijah KoikiTeam 103Arizona State UniversityUpdated October 28, 2026

    Project summary¶

    Team 103 is developing a modular, computer-connected control system for a two-degree-of-freedom robotic arm. Custom PCBs add sensing, communication and motor control to basic DC motors rated no higher than 12 V. My board measures both shafts using optical quadrature encoders, conditions their signals, and uses a PIC18F57Q43 Curiosity Nano to prepare feedback for the main controller. This feedback is intended to support the team's closed-loop motion control and telemetry. See the Team 103 report for the wider system.

    My contribution¶

    I am responsible for the motor encoder subsystem: shaft sensing, analog filtering and amplification, digital edge conditioning, local power regulation, and the encoder PIC's connection to the main board. Connor's main board receives the proposed feedback link; Walker's motor driver board controls motor power. Encoder firmware and physical integration are the next implementation steps.
